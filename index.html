1Subject matter

The subject matter is the set of confidence scores a document-extraction system reports alongside each extracted field, and whether those scores are calibrated — that is, whether the fields it scores 0.9 are correct about 90% of the time.

Calibration matters because it is what a human-review threshold rests on. A system that routes low-confidence extractions to a reviewer is only as safe as the score doing the routing. Where that score is an artefact of the model's output layer rather than a probability, the routing is decorative.

2Responsibilities

The system owner is responsible for the extraction system, for the confidence scores it emits, for the correctness labels supplied, and for any decision taken on the basis of this report.

The auditor is responsible for measuring the relationship between those scores and those labels using the methods in section 3, and for reporting what the measurements show — including where they show that no safe automation threshold exists.

3Basis of the conclusion

Standard, published methods, applied so that every figure in this report can be independently reproduced from the same inputs:

  • Expected and maximum calibration error — the mean and worst gap between claimed confidence and observed accuracy, across ten equal-width confidence bands.
  • Wilson score intervals on each band's observed accuracy, so that an overclaim is reported only where it exceeds sampling noise.
  • Brier score, as an overall measure of the scores' quality.
  • Risk–coverage analysis, identifying the lowest threshold that meets a stated accuracy target at the lower bound of observed accuracy rather than the point estimate.

No proprietary metric is used and no model is retrained. The procedure runs on the standard library alone and can be executed inside the system owner's own environment.

5.1Confident and wrong

The most useful finding first. Of the decisions this system would automate above the recommended threshold, 158 were incorrect — 21.0%.

These are the errors that reach production unflagged, because the system was surest about exactly them. An aggregate error rate can be discounted; a list of the specific fields the model was most confident about and got wrong cannot.

5.3The review threshold

At a 95% accuracy target, no threshold works.

There is no confidence cut-off on this data at which automated decisions reach 95% accuracy at the lower bound — not a high one, not any one. The honest recommendation is that this system should not be automating against a 95% target at all.

Relax the target to 75% and a threshold appears: ≥ 0.95, automating 31.4% of volume at 79.0% observed accuracy, lower bound 76.0%. Note what that means in practice — to reach even 75%, roughly two-thirds of all volume must go to a human.

Thresholds are taken from the lower bound rather than the point estimate. A threshold fitted to the point estimate fits the sample and fails on the next batch.

5.4Where the error concentrates

The aggregate figure hides the finding. Segmented, the damage concentrates — and it concentrates in the fields and document types that cost most when wrong.

6The sample

Everything above describes this sample. Whether it describes production is a separate question, and it is the one most capable of making a rigorous report worthless.

Every statistical claim in sections 4 and 5 quantifies sampling error — how far the observed accuracy might sit from the truth for the population this sample was drawn from. None of them can detect that the sample came from the wrong population. If 200 documents are hand-picked, an immaculate report can be produced about traffic that does not exist.

Three things this check enforces, and they are refusals rather than reassurances:

  • Without a stated production mix, representativeness is reported as UNVERIFIED — not assumed, not quietly omitted. The mix has to come from the system owner; it cannot be inferred from the data they send.
  • A document type present in production but absent from the sample is reported as unmeasured, not as a small number with wide error bars. The audit says nothing about it at all.
  • Segments below 30 observations get a count and no verdict. A confident-looking figure on eleven documents is worse than no figure.

7Inherent limitations

  • Calibration is measured on the sample supplied. If the document mix shifts, the threshold shifts with it. A threshold is a finding about a corpus, not a permanent property of a model.
  • Correctness derives from the labels provided. Where an extracted value is compared against ground truth, only differences of case and whitespace are forgiven — currency symbols, thousands separators and date formats are not normalised, because normalising them would flatter the result.
  • Rows that cannot be parsed are skipped, counted and reported. Nothing is silently dropped.
  • Confidence intervals reflect sampling error only. They do not account for a corpus that is unrepresentative of production traffic.
