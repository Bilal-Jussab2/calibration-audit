1 Subject matter

The subject matter is the set of confidence scores a document-extraction system reports alongside each extracted field, and whether those scores are calibrated — that is, whether the fields it scores 0.9 are correct about 90% of the time.

Calibration matters because it is what a human-review threshold rests on. A system that routes low-confidence extractions to a reviewer is only as safe as the score doing the routing. If the score is an artefact of the model's output layer rather than a probability, the routing is decorative.

2 Responsibilities

The system owner is responsible for the extraction system, for the confidence scores it emits, for the correctness labels supplied, and for any decision taken on the basis of this report.

The auditor is responsible for measuring the relationship between the scores and the labels using the methods set out in section 3, and for reporting what those measurements show — including where they show that no safe automation threshold exists.

3 Basis of the conclusion

Standard, published methods, applied so that every figure in this report can be independently reproduced from the same inputs:

  • Expected and maximum calibration error — the mean and worst gap between claimed confidence and observed accuracy, across ten equal-width confidence bands.
  • Wilson score intervals on each band's observed accuracy, so that an overclaim is only reported where it exceeds sampling noise.
  • Brier score as an overall measure of the scores' quality.
  • Risk–coverage analysis to identify the lowest threshold meeting a stated accuracy target at the lower bound of observed accuracy, not the point estimate.

No proprietary metric is used and no model is retrained. The procedure runs with the standard library alone and can be executed inside the system owner's own environment.

4 Conclusion

Expected calibration error 0.169 — the threshold for usable is 0.02
Miscalibrated

The confidence scores do not mean what they say. Every band above 0.6 claims more than it delivers, and the gap widens as confidence rises — the scores are least trustworthy exactly where the system depends on them most.

Any automation threshold set from these numbers is guesswork.

5.2 The review threshold

At a 95% accuracy target, no threshold works.

There is no confidence cut-off on this data at which automated decisions reach 95% accuracy at the lower bound — not a high one, not any one. The honest recommendation is that this system should not be automating against a 95% target at all.

Relax the target to 75% and a threshold appears: ≥ 0.95, automating 31.4% of volume at 79.0% observed accuracy, lower bound 76.0%. Note what that means in practice — to reach even 75%, roughly two-thirds of all volume must go to a human.

Thresholds are taken from the lower bound rather than the point estimate. A threshold fitted to the point estimate fits the sample and fails on the next batch.

6 Inherent limitations

  • Calibration is measured on the sample supplied. If the document mix shifts, the threshold shifts with it. A threshold is a finding about a corpus, not a permanent property of a model.
  • Correctness derives from the labels provided. Where an extracted value is compared against ground truth, only differences of case and whitespace are forgiven — currency symbols, thousands separators and date formats are not normalised, because normalising them would flatter the result.
  • Rows that cannot be parsed are skipped, counted and reported. Nothing is silently dropped.
  • Confidence intervals reflect sampling error only. They do not account for a corpus that is unrepresentative of production traffic.
