Does the confidence score mean what it says?

AI systems attach a confidence score to their answers, and software uses it to decide what a person checks and what goes straight through. This index records what each maker says about that score, and whether anyone has published evidence that it holds.

The index

Calibration evidence means data showing how often answers at a given confidence turn out to be right, such as a reliability curve or a calibration error figure. An accuracy benchmark does not count: it says how often a system is right overall, not whether its confidence tells you which answers to trust.

How this index works

  1. Every claim is quoted from the maker's own site, linked, and dated. "None found" means nothing public was found on the date shown. It does not mean the data does not exist.
  2. Every entry is open to correction. A maker who publishes evidence, or who thinks an entry is wrong, can write in and the change is made with a date.
  3. Every independent measurement commits its method before any test data is sent, and the maker sees the results at least seven days before they are published.
